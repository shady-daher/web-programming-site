Introduction

The Internet developed gradually from research into computer networking. Important developments included packet switching, ARPANET, TCP/IP, and the connection of many networks into one network of networks.

Internet Timeline

Important events in the development of the Internet.

  1. 1969 - First ARPANET Message

    On October 29, 1969, the first message was sent over ARPANET between UCLA and the Stanford Research Institute. The first characters received were "LO"; the full word "LOGIN" was completed about an hour later.

    Why it mattered: This was an important early demonstration of communication between computers over a network.

    Source: UCLA - Internet First Words

  2. 1970s - TCP/IP Development

    During the 1970s, Vint Cerf, Robert Kahn, and other researchers worked on TCP/IP, a set of communication protocols designed to allow different networks to communicate.

    Why it mattered: TCP/IP became the basic communication system that allowed different networks to work together.

    Source: Internet Society - Brief History of the Internet

  3. 1983 - ARPANET Adopts TCP/IP

    On January 1, 1983, ARPANET changed from the older NCP protocol to TCP/IP.

    Why it mattered: This helped establish TCP/IP as the common protocol system for interconnected networks.

    Source: Internet Society - IANA Timeline

  4. 1983 - DNS Introduced

    The Domain Name System (DNS) was introduced to make it easier for users to reach computers using names instead of numerical IP addresses.

    Why it mattered: DNS made Internet addresses easier for people to remember and use.

    Source: Internet Society - Brief History of the Internet

  5. 1989 - ARPANET Retired

    By 1989, ARPANET was retired as networking continued to expand beyond the original research network.

    Why it mattered: Internet networking had grown into a much larger system of interconnected networks.

    Source: Internet Society - Short History of the Internet

Three Key Internet Technologies

1. Packet Switching
Packet switching divides data into smaller packets that can travel through a network and be reassembled at the destination.
2. TCP/IP
IP provides addressing and routing, while TCP helps provide reliable delivery of data between computers.
3. DNS
DNS translates human-readable domain names into IP addresses so computers can locate the correct destination.
