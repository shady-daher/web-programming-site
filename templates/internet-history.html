Introduction

The Internet developed gradually from research in computer networking. Early work on packet switching and ARPANET showed that computers in different locations could exchange data. Later, TCP/IP allowed separate networks to communicate and became a foundation of the modern Internet.

Internet Timeline

1969: ARPANET sends its first message

On October 29, 1969, UCLA and the Stanford Research Institute attempted to establish a connection over ARPANET. The first transmission sent only "LO" before the system crashed.

Why it mattered: It was an important early demonstration that computers in different locations could communicate through a network.

Source: UCLA

1970s: Development of TCP/IP

During the 1970s, researchers including Vint Cerf and Robert Kahn developed the ideas behind TCP/IP to allow different networks to communicate.

Why it mattered: It provided the foundation for connecting independent networks into a larger network of networks.

Source: Internet Society

1983: ARPANET adopts TCP/IP

On January 1, 1983, ARPANET changed from NCP to TCP/IP.

Why it mattered: TCP/IP provided a common protocol system that allowed different networks to communicate.

Source: Internet Society

1983: Domain Name System is introduced

DNS was developed as the Internet grew and users needed a practical way to use names instead of numerical IP addresses.

Why it mattered: DNS made Internet services easier to use by connecting human-readable names with network addresses.

Source: Internet Society

1989: ARPANET is formally retired

ARPANET was formally retired in 1989 as networking had expanded beyond the original research network.

Why it mattered: By this time, TCP/IP networking had spread to many other networks and the Internet had grown beyond ARPANET.

Source: Internet Society

Three Key Internet Technologies

Packet Switching

Packet switching divides information into smaller packets that can travel through a network and be reassembled at the destination.

TCP/IP

TCP/IP is a suite of networking protocols. IP handles addressing and routing, while TCP helps manage reliable communication.

DNS

DNS translates human-readable domain names into IP addresses, making the Internet easier to use.
