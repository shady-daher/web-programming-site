Introduction

The Internet started from early research in computer networking. ARPANET was one of the first important networks and helped computers in different places communicate with each other. Later, TCP/IP made it possible for different networks to communicate and helped build the Internet we use today.

Internet Timeline

1969: ARPANET sends its first message

On October 29, 1969, UCLA and the Stanford Research Institute tried to communicate through ARPANET. The first message was "LO" before the receiving computer crashed.

Why it mattered: It showed that computers in different locations could communicate through a network.

Source: UCLA

1970s: Development of TCP/IP

During the 1970s, researchers including Vint Cerf and Robert Kahn worked on TCP/IP to allow different networks to communicate.

Why it mattered: TCP/IP helped make it possible to connect separate networks together.

Source: Internet Society

1983: ARPANET adopts TCP/IP

On January 1, 1983, ARPANET changed from NCP to TCP/IP.

Why it mattered: TCP/IP gave networks a common way to communicate.

Source: Internet Society

1983: Domain Name System is introduced

DNS was developed as the Internet grew and people needed an easier way to use names instead of numerical IP addresses.

Why it mattered: DNS made Internet services easier to use by connecting names with network addresses.

Source: Internet Society

1989: ARPANET is formally retired

ARPANET was formally retired in 1989 as networking had grown beyond the original research network.

Why it mattered: By this time, networking had expanded to many other networks and the Internet had grown beyond ARPANET.

Source: Internet Society

Three Key Internet Technologies

Packet Switching

Packet switching breaks information into smaller packets. These packets travel through the network and are put back together when they reach their destination.

TCP/IP

TCP/IP is a group of networking protocols. IP handles addressing and routing, while TCP helps with reliable communication.

DNS

DNS changes domain names into IP addresses. This makes websites and other Internet services easier to access.
