Introduction

The Internet developed gradually from research in computer networking. Early work on packet switching and ARPANET showed that computers in different locations could exchange data. Later, the development and adoption of TCP/IP allowed separate networks to communicate as a network of networks, forming a foundation of the modern Internet.

Internet Timeline

1969 : ARPANET sends its first message

On October 29, 1969, UCLA and the Stanford Research Institute attempted to establish a connection over ARPANET. The first transmission sent only “LO” before the receiving system crashed; the full login was completed about an hour later.

Why it mattered: This was an important early demonstration that computers at different locations could communicate through a packet-switched network.

Source: https://www.lk.cs.ucla.edu/internet_first_words.html

1970s : Development of TCP/IP

During the 1970s, Vint Cerf, Robert Kahn, and other researchers developed the ideas behind TCP/IP to allow different networks to communicate.

Why it mattered: The work established the foundation for connecting independent networks into a larger network of networks.

Source: https://www.internetsociety.org/internet/history-internet/brief-history-internet/

1983 : ARPANET adopts TCP/IP

On January 1, 1983, ARPANET changed from NCP to TCP/IP.

Why it mattered: This transition provided a common protocol suite for networking and became a major foundation of the Internet.

Source: https://www.internetsociety.org/ianatimeline/

1983 : Domain Name System (DNS) is introduced

As the Internet grew, DNS provided a practical way to use names instead of numerical IP addresses.

Why it mattered: DNS made Internet services easier to use by associating human-readable names with network addresses.

Source: https://www.internetsociety.org/internet/history-internet/brief-history-internet/

1989 : ARPANET is formally retired

ARPANET was formally retired in 1989 as networking had expanded beyond the original research network.

Why it mattered: By then, TCP/IP networking had spread to many other networks, so ARPANET was no longer the only important part of the growing Internet.

Source: https://www.internetsociety.org/internet/history-internet/short-history-of-the-internet/

Three Key Internet Technologies

1. Packet Switching

Packet switching divides information into smaller packets that can travel through a network and be reassembled at the destination. It was fundamental to ARPANET and later Internet networking.

2. TCP/IP

TCP/IP is a suite of networking protocols. IP provides addressing and routing, while TCP manages reliable communication and delivery. Together, they made communication between different networks possible.

3. DNS : Domain Name System

DNS translates human-readable domain names into IP addresses. This makes Internet services easier to use because users do not need to remember numerical addresses.
